Astronomy in South Africa

Exploring the Universe From South Africa
South Africa has an important role in modern Astronomy. The country is home to some of the world's impressive astronomical facilities, allowing scientists to observe and study objects in the Universe. South Africa's telescopes also provide an opportunity for learners to discover how Science and technology are used to explore the Universe.

🔭 Southern African Large Telescope (SALT)

Location: Near Sutherland, South Africa Type: Optical telescope The Southern African Large Telescope, commonly known as SALT, is an optical telescope located near Sutherland in the Northern Cape. An optical telescope observes light from astronomical objects. SALT allows scientists to observe and study objects in the Universe and contributes to astronomical research. What learners can discover Learners can explore how optical telescopes work and how astronomers use them to observe objects in space.

📡 MeerKAT Radio Telescope

Location: Near Carnarvon, South Africa

Type: Radio telescope

MeerKAT is a radio telescope located near Carnarvon in the Northern Cape. It allows astronomers to study the Universe by detecting radio waves from objects in space.

What learners can discover

Learners can explore the difference between optical and radio telescopes and learn how scientists use different types of telescopes to investigate the Universe.
